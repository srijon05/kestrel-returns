**To:** Ritu Deshpande **From:** Kabir Nanda's team **Re:** Returns – what the model can and cannot do

**The decision.** Don't hold risky orders. Phone them before dispatch instead – and only the roughly one in three where the call pays for itself. Holding loses money; calling makes it.

**The number.** 95% accuracy is not achievable, and the board should not be told it is. Only about 1 order in 9 comes back, so a "model" that predicts *nothing* is already 88.5% accurate. The best honest model reaches about 90%. (A version scoring 98% exists – it peeks at pickup bookings that are only created *after* a customer asks for a return. It would be useless at the warehouse door.) What the model does well is *ranking*: the riskiest 10% of orders are returned 43% of the time, nearly four times the normal rate. It is tested on April–June 2026 orders it never saw, and its probabilities match reality.

**The rupees** (per quarter, about 2,100 orders; a return costs Rs 1,150, a call Rs 45):
- Returns currently cost roughly **Rs 2.8 lakh** a quarter.
- Calling the ~32% highest-risk orders saves about **Rs 36,000** net (likely range Rs 26,000–46,000) – if calls prevent 35% of returns as your spring pilot suggested. If they prevent only 20%, it is about Rs 12,000, so call a smaller group (~16%). Calling *everyone* breaks even at best.
- Holding the riskiest 175 orders: 12% cancel, so you lose sales on orders that were fine. Unless margin on a lost sale is under about 7%, it **costs** money (about Rs 21,000 at 20% margin).
- Be realistic: this trims returns costs by about 13%, not by half.

**Shield.** Shield members return twice as often (19% vs 9%), so they would be 4 in 10 of the calls. Holds would hit your best customers; a friendly confirmation call is much less risky. Meenal's concern is right – please agree a script with her.
